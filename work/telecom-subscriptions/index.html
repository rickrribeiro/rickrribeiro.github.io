Overview

At Agile Content I worked on the subscription-management platform behind the digital products of the biggest telecom company in Argentina. The platform runs critical, money-moving flows: purchase, billing, renewal and cancellation.

The problem

High-volume transactional services must be reliable, observable and cheap to operate, and every change has to be safe to deploy.

What I did

I developed and maintained microservices — Node.js APIs and serverless functions — took part in defining architecture and project patterns, and created and maintained the infrastructure with Docker, Terraform, the Serverless Framework and CI/CD.

Architecture

Simplified view. Node.js APIs and Lambda functions communicate through SQS, persist to RDS and S3, feed Redshift for analytics and are traced with CloudWatch and X-Ray.

  • Sales channels
  • Node.js APIs
  • Lambda functions
  • SQS
  • RDS
  • S3 → Redshift
  • CloudWatch · X-Ray

Engineering challenges

  1. Designing for high availability in services where every failure affects a customer's subscription.
  2. Making a distributed, event-driven system observable end to end.
  3. Raising test confidence across many small services.

Outcomes

  • AWS-based solutions (Lambda, SQS, CloudWatch, ECS, X-Ray, Parameter Store, S3, Redshift, RDS) supporting millions of events per month.
  • Led internal API-testing libraries built on Robot Framework and reached 100% unit-test coverage, significantly improving deployment confidence.
  • Contributed to architecture decisions and service patterns that improved reliability and reduced incident frequency.
  • Built datasets and reports with Tableau and QuickSight.

Lessons

  • In serverless systems, observability is part of the architecture, not an add-on.
  • Shared testing libraries scale quality better than per-service heroics.
